I reread my April 2023 post. The advice to check the work holds up. The assumption that the useful work would stay small is looking less convincing.

Claude 3.5 Sonnet's release this month is another reason to reassess. The coding capabilities and artifact preview deserve evaluation. I'm not treating a vendor benchmark as a measurement of what happens in a real team's repository.

The more interesting experiment is a change with context. Supply an existing interface, the behavior to preserve, and the awkward constraint. Ask for an approach and a proposed implementation. Evaluate what remains to be corrected.

That is a larger unit of assistance than the standalone function I had in mind last year. It can be valuable even while mistakes remain common enough to require attention.

I don't yet trust ambiguous ownership of a whole application. But I was too quick to let that objection define the limits of useful assistance.

The next concern is review. If a tool produces several plausible changes quickly, somebody still has to understand them. A team can gain drafting capacity and discover that acceptance is the queue. Generated lines won't tell us whether that trade was worthwhile.

I'd keep the experiments bounded, retain the corrections, and measure the complete task. Give people room to find useful workflows without making tool usage a target in itself.

I have revised the expectation. I don't have a staffing forecast.
